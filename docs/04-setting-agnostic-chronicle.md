# The Heroic Chronicle — Setting-Agnostic Edition

A collaborative backstory generator for any fantasy world. A player and their DM work through it together to find out where a character comes from, who they love and hate, what shaped them, and what they're destined to do.

This edition keeps the original system's **dice and odds exactly as they were**. Only the world has been removed. Regions, nations and settlements are now **archetypes**: roles that every fantasy world has some version of, which you map onto your own setting.

> **Rules edition: 5.5e.** This chronicle uses the **2024 revised core rules**. Where the original relied on a 2014 rule, name or table that no longer exists, it has been updated to its closest 2024 equivalent. Every such change is marked **[5.5e]** in the text and listed in the [5.5e change log](#55e-change-log) at the end. Unmarked results are unchanged.

> **Abbreviations:** **PH** = *Player's Handbook* (2024) · **DMG** = *Dungeon Master's Guide* (2024) · **MM** = *Monster Manual* (2025) · **CR** = challenge rating

## 1. How to use this chronicle

The chronicle has two halves:

| Half | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Backstory** | Places your character in the world: homeland, settlement, family, allies, rivals, formative events, a favourite food and a lingering secret. Some results give **real mechanical benefits**: proficiencies, feats, spells, magic items or companions. |
| **Prophecy** | You write **three goals** for your character: immediate, long-term and end-of-campaign. Completing each one earns a reward. |

**Ground rules:**

- **Roll or choose.** You can roll for every step, choose every result, or mix the two. If a result doesn't suit your character, reroll it or pick something else.
- **Let it surprise you.** The chronicle works best if you start without a fixed concept. If a roll makes you rethink your character, go with it.
- **Work with your DM.** Many results create NPCs and plot hooks. Your DM will want to know about them, and may make some rolls in secret.
- **DMs can use it too.** Run a villain or major NPC through the chronicle and they'll feel just as grounded as the heroes.

## 2. Fitting it to your world

Every place in this chronicle is an **archetype**. Before rolling, the DM decides which part of their world plays each role. A world doesn't need all of them. If one is missing, **merge it into the nearest match** or reroll results that land on it.

### The five regions

| Region archetype | What it is | Map it to… |
|---|---|---|
| **The Free Coast** | A chain of wealthy, independent port cities that prize freedom and trade. Pirates prowl its waters. | Your world's merchant republics, city-states or trading coast |
| **The Heartland Valleys** | The fertile, settled interior of a great empire: farmland, river towns, mining cities and industry | The rural or industrial heart of your largest kingdom |
| **The Imperial Plains** | The empire's core, home to its capital and seat of power | The same kingdom's capital region |
| **The Untamed Wilds** | Cold, rugged frontier beyond the empire's reach, where outlaws rule and an ancient stronghold endures | Your world's badlands, northern forests or lawless frontier |
| **The Rival Realm** | A nation across the border, culturally distinct and at odds with the empire, often at war with it | Your empire's chief rival, a "monstrous" kingdom, or a hidden underground realm |

### The six societies

Each region is governed by one or two **societies**. They determine how your background is viewed (Step 3).

| Society | Region | Values |
|---|---|---|
| **The Free League** | Free Coast | Personal freedom |
| **The Pirate Fleets** | Free Coast | Freedom taken to a violent extreme |
| **The Empire** | Heartland Valleys, Imperial Plains | Crown and country above personal freedom |
| **The Outlaw Clans** | Untamed Wilds | Strength; crime is a virtue and mercy a weakness |
| **The Old Hold** | Untamed Wilds | Order, tradition and craft |
| **The Dynasty** | Rival Realm | Knowledge and spiritual enlightenment |
| **The Deep City** | Rival Realm | A splinter power, hostile even to the Dynasty |

**Example mappings:**

- *Classic high fantasy:* the Free Coast is a league of elven and human port cities, the Empire is the human kingdom, the Wilds are the orc-haunted north, and the Rival Realm is a dwarven empire under the mountains.
- *Dark fantasy:* the Empire is a theocracy, the Free Coast its heretical colonies, the Dynasty a kingdom of the undead, and the Deep City a cult hidden beneath it.

### Names

Settlements are listed by **type and character**, not by name. Use a matching settlement from your world, or invent one when it's rolled. If more than one player rolls the same entry, they share a hometown.

## 3. The process at a glance

```
 HOMELAND (d100) ──► BACKGROUND (d20) ──► SOCIAL STATUS
        │                                     │  gives 0–2 "acquired" allies/rivals
        ▼                                     │  based on background × society
 HOME SETTLEMENT (d100, per region)           │
        │                                     │
        ▼                                     │
 SPECIES (settlement demographics)            │
        │                                     │
        ▼                                     │
 FAMILY SIZE (d100: parents + siblings)       │
        │                                     │
        ▼                                     │
 FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS (d3 of them, d100 each) │
   each = +1 ally or +1 rival                 │
                                              ▼
                        ACQUIRED ALLIES & RIVALS
                          • relationship (d100)
                          • identity / stat block (d100)
                                    │
                    powerful identity? ──► +1 FATEFUL MOMENT (d20)
                                                 │
                                    proficiencies, feats, items, etc.
        ▼

        ▼

        ▼
```

> **Fateful moments**, which carry most of the mechanical rewards, come **only** from rolling a powerful (★) identity for an *acquired* ally or rival.

| d100 | Region | Society (choose where offered) |

## Step 3 — Social Status

Your background doesn't change, but **how your society views your background** sets your standing, and with it how many allies and rivals you start with. Social status isn't fixed. Adventurers can rebel against it and change it over time.

| Society | Values | Who's respected | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Free League** | Personal freedom | Folk heroes (Farmers), entertainers, celebrities and iconoclasts. The working sailor symbolises the League's strength. | Sailors hate the Pirate Fleets. A notable sailor may have a pirate rival. |

| **The Empire** | Crown and country above personal freedom | Rural folk are seen as honest and pious. Nobles are seen as benevolent or tyrannical. | Laws bend for the rich. Joining the imperial army and following the state religion are the most patriotic acts. |

## Step 5 — Species

Each settlement in your world has its own mix of peoples. The DM gives the settlement's demographics as percentages. **Roll d100 against that list, or just choose.** An "other species" result lets you pick any species not already listed for that settlement.

> **[5.5e]** "Race" is now **species**. Your species no longer gives ability score increases; those come from your background (Step 2).

## Step 6 — Family

Roll **d100 twice** on the table for your settlement's size: once for living parents, once for living siblings.

| Village d100 | City d100 | Living parents | Living siblings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01–10 | 01–05 | 3 or more | 2d4 + 2 |
| 11–50 | 06–60 | 2 | 2d4 |
| 51–89 | 61–80 | 1 | 1d4 |
| 90–00 | 81–00 | 0 | 0 |

- **Village families tend to be larger:** farms need hands. **City families are smaller:** cities are expensive.
- In families with three or more children, one (usually the eldest) inherits the family trade. The rest are pushed outward into apprenticeships, scholarships or, for wealthy families, a prestigious arcane academy.
- **"Parents"** can mean any household that raised you: polyamorous families, multiple spouses, communal upbringing. **"Siblings"** can include half-siblings, cousins or sworn kin.
- **Choose each family member's gender and age.**

## Step 8 — Acquired Allies & Rivals

These are the allies and rivals from **Step 3**. For each one:

1. Roll on **Ally Relationships** or **Rival Relationships**, or invent one with the DM.
2. Roll on **Ally and Rival Identities** to give them a stat block.
3. If the identity is marked ★, gain **one fateful moment** (Step 9).

### Ally Relationships (d100)

| d100 | Relationship |
|---|---|
| 01–10 | **A loyal pet.** Skip the identity roll and choose any beast of **CR 1/8 or lower**. |
| 11–20 | They lost a bet to you and still can't pay, so they're repaying you in favours. |
| 21–30 | A former beggar you gave a large sum to. Their life turned around and they want to repay you. |
| 31–40 | Your favourite drinking partner. Their home is open to you and your friends. |
| 41–50 | Your old mentor. You left partway through your training and can return to finish it when you're ready. |

## Step 9 — Fateful Moments

Roll **d20** once for each fateful moment you earned in Step 8. Or skip the roll and design the moment with your DM.

- **Duplicate proficiencies:** if you already have the proficiency or training, choose another of **the same type** (armour training, skill, language, tool or weapon).
- **Doesn't fit?** If a result contradicts your backstory (siblings you don't have, say), reroll or adapt it with your DM.

| d20 | Event | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your parents were murdered in front of you. Roll on the Identities table for the killer. | Proficiency in **Stealth** and **Survival** |
| 2 | You found a dying dark elf wearing a talisman that held a portrait of their child and a name you'd never heard. | **Amulet of proof against detection and location** |
| 3 | Lost in the wilderness, you were saved by a stranger's gift. Roll on the Identities table for the stranger. | **One uncommon magic item** (DM's choice or random; reroll consumables) **[5.5e]** |
| 4 | You survived a terrible storm, and ever since you've had dream visions from an unknown god. | Proficiency in **Arcana** *or* **Religion** |
| 5 | A famous warrior trained you in their signature weapon. | **Martial weapon** proficiency, one such weapon (give it a history or quirk using the DMG's magic item special features), and the **Savage Attacker** feat **[5.5e]** |
| 6 | You were the only survivor when monsters raided your home. | Proficiency in **Stealth** *or* **martial weapons** |
| 7 | A famous mage tutored you. | A **spellbook** and the **Magic Initiate (Wizard)** feat **[5.5e]** |
| 8 | You spent a year with a travelling circus. | Proficiency in **Acrobatics** *or* **Performance**, plus a **disguise kit** |
| 9 | You spent a year magically transformed into a bear, until a druid broke the spell. | Cast ***speak with animals*** **at will** |
| 10 | You were press-ganged into an army and the war left you shaken. | **Medium armour training, shield training** and proficiency with **martial weapons** **[5.5e]**, plus a lasting **madness**† |
| 11 | Bandits kidnapped you. You escaped with an old thief's help. | Proficiency with **thieves' tools** and in **Stealth** |
| 12 | A demon lord visited you in a dream. | Cast ***find familiar*** as a ritual (the familiar is always a **quasit**, using its MM stat block), plus a lasting **madness**† |
| 13 | Evil lycanthropes attacked you in a remote forest, and you barely escaped. | **Cursed:** wereboar, wererat or werewolf (DM's choice)‡ |
| 14 | Lost in a forest or jungle, you were saved by a werebear or weretiger who believed you were destined for greatness. | **Lycanthropy (good)**, granted with your consent‡ |
| 15 | You saved a pseudodragon from a giant spider. | A **pseudodragon companion**. The DM controls it, and it obeys you as long as you treat it well. |
| 16 | You nearly died of disease (cackle fever, sewer plague or sight rot). A travelling scholar-monk helped you survive. | A **periapt of health**, which keeps the still-present disease in check |
| 17 | You were exiled or imprisoned for a crime, guilty or not. | Proficiency in **Intimidation**, and you know the **Thieves' Cant** language **[5.5e]** |
| 18 | You saved a riderless, fully saddled horse from wolves. | A **riding horse and saddle**, and proficiency in **Animal Handling** |
| 19 | You found a treasure map tucked inside a parent's mysterious book. | A **treasure map** to a place the DM chooses |
| 20 | A letter revealed that you're the secret child of a noble family in the capital. | **100 gp** and a **signet ring** of your true family |

† **Madness [5.5e].** The 2014 DMG's random indefinite madness table isn't part of the 2024 rules. Your DM gives you a lasting flaw instead, such as a fear, compulsion or delusion. It lasts until it's cured by magic like *greater restoration*, or through the story.

‡ **Lycanthropy [5.5e].** The 2014 MM had specific rules for player-character lycanthropes; the 2025 MM doesn't. Your DM decides how the curse works for you, using the lycanthrope's stat block as a guide: which traits and forms you gain, and how much control you have when you change.

## Step 10 — Favourite Food

Roll **d8** for your home region, or choose or invent one. This is purely for flavour.

| Region | Character of the cuisine | Signature drinks |

## Step 12 — Prophecy

### Writing your goals

1. Write **three goals**. Each has two parts:
   - **The goal:** what your character wants to achieve.
   - **The complication:** what happens once they achieve it, good or bad.
2. Each goal has a different timescale:

| Goal | Timescale |
|---|---|
| **Immediate** | Early in the campaign |
| **Long-term** | Mid-campaign |
| **Arc-ending** | Concludes your character's story at the end of the campaign |

3. You only need the **immediate** goal to start. Write the others once you have a feel for the campaign.
4. Linking a goal to your **Mysterious Secret** works well. You can also write goals with your DM so they tie into the story the DM is planning.
5. When you complete a goal it takes on a **prophetic tone**: you've done what you were driven, or destined, to do. Your character earns a **reward**.

### Prophecy Inspirations (d20, optional)

Roll for ideas, then adjust the goal to suit your character, or roll again.

| d20 | Goal → Complication |
|---|---|
| 1 | Kill the creature that killed my parents → and lose my sense of purpose |
| 2 | Learn why a council of powerful mages took my sibling → setting uncontrollable political events in motion |
| 3 | Save my village from a year of gnoll raids → which inspires me to greater heroism |
| 4 | Uncover the secrets of a rival nation's sacred mysteries → and learn a terrifying truth |
| 5 | Join an order of monster hunters → and gain power at a steep price |
| 6 | Steal a fortune from a pirate lord → and attract unsavoury people with my wealth |
| 7 | Become a hero of the war between the great nations → and be haunted by the atrocities I saw |
| 8 | Cleanse the corruption of a blighted forest → by taking it into myself |
| 9 | Infiltrate a vast criminal syndicate → and be forced to commit evil acts |
| 10 | Find a relic in the frozen wastes and become famous → but the relic exacts a terrible cost |
| 11 | Speak with a dragon and survive → and earn its eternal wrath |
| 12 | Steal a holy relic from a foreign temple → and be hunted by everyone who wants its power |
| 13 | I will hesitate at a crucial moment → and someone else will suffer for it |
| 14 | Stumble into a secret society → reject it, but be drawn back in |
| 15 | Befriend and ride a flying beast → and others will envy the bond |
| 16 | Kneel before a foreign queen without knowing who she is → and be led into danger |
| 17 | Hold the fate of the realm in my hands, briefly, before its heir → and realise the stakes too late |
| 18 | Cause the death of a powerful merchant prince → know who the real killer is, and have no one believe me |
| 19 | Anger a powerful outlaw clan → and others will suffer as they hunt me |
| 20 | Meet my birth parents → and learn a world-changing secret about my birth |

### Pacing (for the DM)

> **Sessions per goal ≈ expected campaign length in sessions ÷ total prophecy goals across all players**
>
> *Example: a 60-session campaign with 5 players × 3 goals = 15 goals, so about one goal completed every 4 sessions.*

If goals are completed faster than that, that's usually fine. Players would rather see their own stories come up often than wade through plot that isn't about them.

### Prophecy rewards

The DM chooses one of these, or something of similar strength:

| Reward | Duration |
|---|---|
| Gain **Heroic Inspiration** whenever you finish a long rest **[5.5e]** | 1d10 days |
| **Advantage** on saving throws to avoid or end the **Frightened** condition | 1d10 days |
| Weapon attacks deal an extra **1d6 damage** of the weapon's type | 1d4 days |

### Things to check at your table

- **Background bonuses stack with the chronicle.** Every character now starts with an origin feat from their background. The feats from fateful moments #5 and #7 are *extra* on top of that, just as in the 2014 version they were extra on top of a background feature.
- **Weapon Mastery.** The #5 signature weapon doesn't give you its mastery property unless your class already has Weapon Mastery. If you want #5 to feel more like a signature weapon, a DM could let you use that one weapon's mastery property instead of taking Savage Attacker.
